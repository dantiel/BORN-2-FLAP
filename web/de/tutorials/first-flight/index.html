Erster Flug

Abheben, oben bleiben.

An ornithopter does not want to fall — it wants to fly, and in many ways it is more stable than a fixed-wing. Thrust, lift and control all live in one moving surface, so a flapping wing steers and recovers throughout the beat: it shakes off a gust or a stall that would drop a rigid plane, and the same unsteady lift lets it slow down without losing its footing in the air.

Anticipate the beat

A servo ornithopter flies on a strict budget. Each wingbeat is a fixed impulse — once the servos reach their limit they push no harder, no matter how hard you push the stick. So you fly by timing, not force: choose the next stroke before the manoeuvre, climb on the beat you planned, and glide while the wing rests. Anticipation is the throttle you actually have.

Launch

Rest on the ground, hold Space for a hand launch — 8.5 m/s forward, a touch upward. The launch cannot repeat mid-air; commit.

Flap and glide

Hold W for continuous flapping at cruise effort. Shift + W demands the full stroke. Release to glide — airspeed and height are spent, never free.

Turn and climb

A / D banks you. Up / Down raises and lowers the nose — pulling up costs airspeed, so trade speed for height like the wing you are flying.

Land

Slow down, keep the nose up, and let the ground meet you gently. R resets body and firmware state.
